# Part 2 — Consumer Tension Library

A **tension** is the small, true friction a real person feels in a real moment. It's the raw material for hooks. A good tension:

- is said in the **customer's words**, not ours
- is **true at the size it actually is** (no inflation)
- carries an **emotion** (mild guilt, annoyance, doubt, amusement, desire), not just a fact
- points to a **moment** we can film
- can be resolved by our product **without a health claim**

Each tension is coded `T-##` so briefs, angles and test logs can reference it.

> The examples in the original brief ("Healthy snacks nobody actually wants to eat", "I bought it because the front said healthy…", etc.) are treated as *already known*. Everything below is additional.

### How to read the table

- **Tension**: how the customer would say it
- **What's really going on**: the insight underneath, which is what the ad actually has to resolve
- **Resolve with**: the framework step(s) that do the work
- **Product**: best fit
- **Watch out**: the tone or claim risk

## 1. Parents

| Code | Tension | What's really going on | Resolve with | Product | Watch out |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| T-01 | "I've become a label detective in the supermarket aisle and I still don't know what half the words mean." | Parents *want* to read labels but feel under-qualified. The pain isn't the label, it's the **uncertainty**. A short list they can understand removes that. | Step 9 (back-of-pack) | Breakfast Cookies, Spread | Don't mock other labels' ingredients as "chemicals". |
| T-02 | "I'm the one saying 'no' to snacks all day. I'd love to just say 'yes' for once." | Parents are tired of being the snack police. The emotional payoff is **being the yes-parent without second-guessing**. | Steps 6, 14 | Chocolate Cookies, Spread | Don't imply other snacks deserve a "no". |
| T-03 | "Grandparents give them whatever. I can't fight that battle every weekend." | Intergenerational tension about treats. A product that grandparents *and* parents approve of ends the tug-of-war. | Steps 2, 14 | Chocolate Cookies, Breakfast Cookies | Keep it affectionate. Grandparents are loved, not villains. |
| T-04 | "I bought the 'kids' version' because it had a cartoon on it. Then I realised the cartoon was the main ingredient." | Kid-marketing on packs makes parents feel manipulated. We win by being the **adult-looking** pack that kids still like. | Steps 5, 9 | Chocolate Cookies | Playful, not accusatory. |
