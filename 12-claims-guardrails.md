# Part 13 — Claims and Brand Guardrails

**Credibility is our competitive advantage.** A brand built on "read the back of the pack" can't afford one overclaim. A single misleading ad undermines the whole positioning.

> ⚖️ **Not legal advice.** This is a working guide for the creative team. Indian food advertising is governed mainly by the **FSSAI (Advertising and Claims) Regulations, 2018** and the FSSAI labelling regulations. Self-regulation is by **ASCI** (Advertising Standards Council of India), including its influencer and food-and-beverage guidelines. Platforms add their own rules (**Meta Advertising Standards**). Infant foods are additionally governed by the **IMS Act, 1992**. Thresholds and wording in these rules change. **Every yellow claim and every regulatory point marked "verify" must be confirmed with a qualified food-regulatory advisor against the current regulations.**

## The traffic-light system

| Colour | Meaning | Who can approve |
|---|---|---|
| 🟢 **GREEN** | Factual, verifiable from the current pack, no implied benefit. Safe to use. | Creative lead |
| 🟡 **YELLOW** | Permissible **only** with documented substantiation (current pack, lab report, regulatory check). Wording must be precise. | Creative lead + documented evidence + (first use) regulatory review |
| 🔴 **RED** | Don't use. Legal risk, platform risk or brand-credibility risk. | No one |

## 🟢 GREEN: safe communication

**Ingredient facts (as printed on the current pack):**
- "Made with millets and oats" (Breakfast Cookies)
- "Sweetened with jaggery" (Breakfast Cookies, Ragi & Cocoa Spread)
- "Made with cow butter" (Breakfast Cookies)
- "Peanuts, ragi, cocoa, jaggery" (Spread, *as core ingredients; full list must match pack*)
- "Sprouted ragi and sweet potato" (Sunrise Bowl, *as core ingredients*)
- "Here's the full ingredient list" + the verbatim list

**Formulation facts (as printed, and verified):**
- "No maida" · "No palm oil" (Breakfast Cookies)
- "No added sugar" (Unsweetened PB, *see the sugar section*)
- "25g protein per 100g" (Unsweetened PB, *stated as a number, from the pack*)

**Taste, experience and occasion:**
- "Crunchy", "chocolatey", "peanut-forward", "tastes like roasted peanuts" (descriptive and demonstrable)
- "Great with chai", "tiffin-friendly", "for the office drawer", "on toast or roti"
- "Our kids love it" (only as a **real** personal statement from a real creator/customer)

**Brand and values:**
- "Consciously Sourced. Passionately Crafted."
- "Familiar foods, rethought."
- "Read the back of the pack."
- "Made with ingredients you recognise." *(Only if every ingredient on the pack is plainly recognisable. Check each product.)*
- "We chose jaggery over refined sugar."

## 🟡 YELLOW: use only if substantiated

| Claim | Condition | Suggested safe wording |
|---|---|---|
| "No refined sugar" | Verified formulation; only if **no** refined sugar in any ingredient (e.g. chocolate chips often contain sugar). Must not imply sugar-free. | "No refined sugar. Sweetened with jaggery." |
| "No preservatives" | Verified formulation, **and** regulatory check that the claim is permitted and not misleading for this category (a "free from" claim can mislead if such products don't normally contain it). | "No added preservatives" *(verify preferred regulatory wording)* |
| "High protein" / "Rich in protein" / "Source of protein" | Product must meet FSSAI nutrient-content claim thresholds *(verify current thresholds and basis)*. | Prefer the plain fact: "25g protein per 100g" |
| "Source of fibre" / "High fibre" | Must meet FSSAI thresholds per 100g *(verify with the nutrition panel)*. | State the number: "[X]g fibre per 100g" |
| "Natural" / "made with natural ingredients" | FSSAI restricts "natural". It generally applies to single foods with minimal processing, while composite foods may only refer to "made from natural ingredients" under conditions *(verify)*. | Avoid. Use "ingredients you recognise" |
| "Traditional", "authentic", "pure", "fresh", "real", "original" | These words are specifically regulated *(verify conditions)*. Note: our brand name "Puŕ" is a trademark, not a claim. **Don't use "pure" as a descriptive claim** in copy. | "Inspired by ingredients our grandparents cooked with" |
| "Clean label" | Not a regulated term, but can mislead if it implies other products are "unclean". Use to describe **our approach**, never as a comparison. | "Our clean-label approach: short, readable ingredient lists" |
| "Only X ingredients" / "Single ingredient" | Count must exactly match the current pack. | "[N] ingredients. Here they are." |
| "Low sugar" / "Low fat" | Must meet FSSAI thresholds *(verify)*; unlikely for cookies or spread. | Avoid unless verified |
| Comparative claims ("less sugar than regular spreads") | Needs a lab-verified, like-for-like, representative comparison; must not disparage; regulatory review. | Prefer "Read yours. Then read ours." |
| Reviews, ratings, customer counts | Must be real, current, sourced (platform + date) and representative. | "Rated [X]★ by [N] customers on [platform], [month year]" |
| Traceability / sourcing claims | The traceability system must be live, accurate and linked from the current pack. | "Scan the jar to see where the peanuts came from." |
| "Made for kids" / "Kids' snack" | Triggers ASCI's heightened standards for ads targeting children *(verify)*; no pester-power, no encouraging excessive consumption. | "Tiffin-friendly" |
| Prep time ("ready in 5 minutes") | Must match pack instructions and be demonstrable on camera. | "[X] minutes, as per pack instructions" |
| Taste superiority ("tastes better than…") | Puffery risk; needs a consumer test if specific. | Show real reactions instead |

## 🔴 RED: never use

- Disease claims: diabetes, cholesterol, heart disease, blood pressure, PCOS, cancer, "prevents", "treats", "cures", "manages"
- "Diabetic-friendly", "safe for diabetics", "low GI", "doesn't spike blood sugar"
- Immunity claims: "boosts immunity", "strengthens immunity"
- Child growth/development: "for growth", "helps kids grow taller/stronger", "brain development", "bone development", "concentration", "better grades"
- Energy/performance: "boosts energy", "sustained energy", "stamina", "keeps you active all day", "pre-workout", "recovery", "builds muscle"
- Digestion: "easy to digest", "gut health", "improves digestion", "gentle on the tummy", "prebiotic benefits of sprouting"
- Weight: "weight loss", "helps you lose weight", "fat burning", "keeps you slim", "diet snack"
- "Healthy" as a product claim ("healthy cookie", "healthy breakfast")
- "Guilt-free", "sin-free", "zero guilt", "cheat-day approved"
- "Superfood", "detox", "cleanse", "nutrient-dense powerhouse"
- "Sugar-free" or "zero sugar" (for any product containing jaggery or natural sugars)
- "Complete breakfast", "balanced meal", "meal replacement", "all the nutrition you need"
- Fear language about other foods: "toxic", "poison", "chemicals", "junk", "harmful additives", "slow poison"
- Named or recognisable competitor comparisons, or showing competitor packs
- Invented reviews, numbers, experts, certifications or founder stories
- "Recommended by doctors / nutritionists / paediatricians" (unless genuinely true, documented and permitted)
- Before/after body, weight or health imagery
- Any promotion of infant food for children under 2 without legal clearance (see below)
- Targeting or copy that implies knowledge of personal health attributes (Meta prohibits e.g. "Are you diabetic?", "Struggling with your weight?")

## Topic-by-topic guidance

### Protein
- ✅ "25g protein per 100g" (PB, a fact from the pack)
- 🟡 "High protein" / "rich in protein": only if FSSAI thresholds are met *(verify)*
- 🟡 Per-serving protein: only using the pack's stated serving size *(verify)*
- 🔴 "Builds muscle", "for gains", "recovery", "protein your body needs"
- 🔴 Implying the cookies or spread are protein foods (unless the panel supports it)
- **Tip:** a number is more credible *and* safer than an adjective.

### Fibre
- 🟡 Only state if the nutrition panel shows a value that meets the threshold *(verify for each product)*
- 🔴 "Good for digestion", "keeps you regular", "gut-friendly"
- Don't transfer the fibre content of millets/oats/ragi in general to our product.

### Sugar
| Product | Safe wording | Never |
|---|---|---|
| Breakfast Cookies | "No refined sugar. Sweetened with jaggery." | "Sugar-free", "no sugar", "no added sugar" (jaggery *is* added sugar) |
| Chocolate Cookies | *(verify sweetener first)* | Any sugar claim before verification |
| Unsweetened PB | "No added sugar" | "Sugar-free", "zero sugar" (peanuts have naturally occurring sugars) |
| Ragi & Cocoa Spread | "Sweetened with jaggery" | "Low sugar", "no added sugar", "sugar-free" |
| Sunrise Bowl | *(verify whether any sweetener)* | Any sugar claim before verification |

- 🔴 "Jaggery is healthier than sugar", "jaggery doesn't raise blood sugar", "jaggery is good for diabetics". These are all RED.
- ✅ Being honest about jaggery is a **trust asset**: "Yes, jaggery is still sugar. We chose it over refined sugar."
- **"No added sugar" (regulatory note):** this claim generally requires that no sugars *of any kind* are added, including sugar-containing ingredients *(verify current FSSAI conditions)*. So it applies to the Unsweetened PB, but **not** to products with jaggery.

### Child nutrition
- ✅ "Tiffin-friendly", "kids enjoy it" (as real reactions), "ingredients parents can read"
- 🟡 Anything positioning a product as *for* children: apply ASCI's standards for advertising to children *(verify)*; don't encourage excessive consumption, pester-power ("ask your parents to buy") or undermining parental authority.
- 🔴 Growth, height, strength, brain, bones, immunity, concentration, "nutrition your child needs", "complete nutrition"
- 🔴 Any implication that parents who don't buy it are failing their child

### Growth
- 🔴 In any form, for any product. This includes the **"PurGro" name**, which requires legal review before launch for implied growth claims (see [PurGro](products/06-purgro-and-future-products.md)).

### Energy
- ✅ Occasion language: "for the 4 PM break", "mid-morning snack"
- 🔴 "Energy", "energising", "boost", "stamina", "fuel your day", "sustained release", "keeps you going"
- Note: "fuel" metaphors are an implied energy claim. Avoid them.

### Digestion
- 🔴 Everything. Including "sprouted for easy digestion" (Sunrise Bowl) and "fibre for digestion".
- ✅ "Sprouted ragi" as a factual process description, with no stated benefit.

### Weight loss
- 🔴 Everything, including implied: "light", "won't make you fat", "for your diet", "guilt-free", "snack smart to stay slim".
- Don't target weight-loss interests with body-focused creative.

### Diabetes
- 🔴 Everything. Don't mention it, target it, or imply it ("sugar-conscious" + jaggery can imply it; be careful).
- If customers ask in comments: reply with the facts (ingredients, nutrition panel) and suggest consulting their doctor. Never give suitability advice.

### Immunity
- 🔴 Everything, for every product.

### Disease claims
- 🔴 Everything. Food ads can't claim to prevent, treat or cure any disease.

### "Healthy"
- 🔴 As a product descriptor ("healthy cookies").
- 🟡 In the **customer's voice** about the category, not about us: "Every 'healthy' snack I tried tasted like cardboard" is acceptable as a *tension* line, because it describes the customer's past experience rather than claiming anything about our product. Use sparingly, and never follow it with "…but ours is healthy".
- ✅ Use instead: "better ingredients", "ingredients you can read", "made differently", "rethought".

### "Guilt-free"
- 🔴 Never. It implies food should cause guilt (contrary to our non-preachy positioning), and implies a health or weight benefit.
- ✅ Alternatives: "A treat you don't have to explain", "Snack without the speech", "Say yes to this one".

### "Natural"
- 🟡/🔴 Regulated. Avoid as a descriptor. See the yellow table.
- ✅ Use: "ingredients you recognise", "things you'd find in your kitchen" (only if true).

### "Clean"
- 🟡 "Clean label" describes our *approach* (short, readable ingredient lists). OK in brand communication.
- 🔴 "Clean eating", "eat clean", or implying others are "dirty/unclean".

### No preservatives
- 🟡 Only if (1) the formulation contains no preservatives, (2) shelf life is achieved without them, and (3) a regulatory check confirms the claim is permitted and not misleading for the category *(verify)*.
- 🔴 Implying preservatives are dangerous ("no harmful preservatives").

### No refined sugar
- 🟡 Only if **every** ingredient is free of refined sugar (check compound ingredients like chocolate).
- Always pair with the actual sweetener: "No refined sugar. Sweetened with jaggery."
- 🔴 Using it to imply low sugar or sugar-free.

## Sunrise Bowl and infant-food regulation

The **IMS Act, 1992** restricts the promotion of infant foods (for children up to 2 years). FSSAI also has specific regulations for foods for infant nutrition.

**Before any Sunrise Bowl ad:**
1. Confirm the **age group on the pack** and the product's **licensed category**.
2. Get written legal advice on whether and how the product can be advertised.
3. If it's for under-2s: **no paid advertising** until cleared.
4. Never show infants. Never compare to breast milk or suggest it replaces breastfeeding.
5. Keep to ingredient transparency, convenience and routine, with **zero** nutrition, development or health claims.

## Brand-tone guardrails

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Show the back of the pack | Shout claims on the front |
| Let taste lead | Lead with "health" |
| Be honest about trade-offs (jaggery is sugar) | Pretend we're perfect |
| Use gentle humour, with people | Mock people's food choices or traditional foods |
| Celebrate Indian food rituals | Treat chai-biscuit, mithai or pakoras as "bad" |
| Show diverse Indian families (dads, grandparents, working parents) | Always show the mother as the only food decision-maker |
| Say "made differently", "rethought", "ingredients you can read" | Say "healthy", "guilt-free", "clean eating", "superfood" |
| Show real reactions | Script or fake enthusiasm |
| Speak in the customer's voice | Lecture in the brand's voice |
| Stay calm, since the brand is the calm voice in a noisy wellness feed | Use fear, urgency or outrage |

## Platform and disclosure rules (summary)

- **Meta:** no implying personal attributes (health conditions, weight), no before/after body imagery, no misleading claims. Children in ads must follow Meta policy.
- **ASCI influencer guidelines:** all paid or gifted creator content must carry a clear, upfront disclosure (e.g. "#ad", "Paid partnership") *(verify current format requirements)*.
- **Testimonials:** real, with consent, not edited to change meaning, and **no health claims even if the customer said them**.
- **AI content:** label where required; never present an AI avatar's "experience" as a real person's testimonial.
