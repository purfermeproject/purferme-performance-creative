# Part 6 — Static Ad System

Statics are fast to make, cheap to test, and often the best way to find a winning **message** before spending on video. Treat statics as **message tests**: if a headline wins as a static, it will usually win as a video hook too.

## Universal static rules

| Rule | Standard |
|---|---|
| **Formats** | 1:1 (1080×1080) for Feed, 4:5 (1080×1350) as the preferred Feed size, 9:16 (1080×1920) for Stories/Reels. Design 4:5 first and keep critical content inside the 9:16 safe zone (avoid the top 14% and bottom 20%). |
| **One focal point** | Food texture, a face, or a headline. Never three competing elements. |
| **The pack is always recognisable** | Name readable at phone size, unless the format is deliberately "no-pack" (meme). Even then, show it in the end frame or logo corner. |
| **Headline length** | ≤ 8 words ideal, ≤ 12 maximum. |
| **Text coverage** | Keep on-image text to roughly 20% of the area for most formats (legibility and performance, not a platform rule). Typographic formats are the exception. |
| **Brand palette** | Warm, earthy, premium: off-white, jaggery brown, millet gold, leaf green *(use the official brand guide; requires verification)*. Avoid clinical white and "health-food green". |
| **Photography** | Real Indian settings: steel tiffins, kulhads, wooden boards, granite counters, natural window light. No Western stock. |
| **Claims** | Every piece of text passes the [guardrails](12-claims-guardrails.md). |
| **Primary text (caption)** | Line 1 is the second hook, because only ~125 characters show before "…more". Put the swap or proof there. |

## The 17 static formats

Each format includes: headline formula · visual hierarchy · body copy · proof · product placement · CTA · text quantity · what makes it scroll-stopping.

### 1. Problem-first static
- **Headline formula:** "[Relatable moment/friction in customer words]."
  → *"5 PM happens. Every day."*
- **Visual hierarchy:** ① Headline (top 40%) → ② moment photo (a desk with an empty wrapper, a clock) → ③ pack (bottom right)
- **Body copy:** One line of resolution: "Be ready with millets, oats and jaggery in the drawer."
- **Proof:** A small ingredient strip at the bottom.
- **Product placement:** Bottom-right third, 25–30% of the frame.
- **CTA:** "Stock the drawer."
- **Text quantity:** Low-medium (headline + 1 line).
- **Scroll-stopping because:** Instant self-recognition. The viewer sees their own moment before seeing an ad.

### 2. Product-first static
- **Headline formula:** "[Product name]. [Swap in 3–5 words]."
  → *"Breakfast Cookies. Millets, oats, jaggery, butter."*
- **Visual hierarchy:** ① Hero pack + product (60% of frame) → ② headline → ③ 3 icons
- **Body copy:** None or one line.
- **Proof:** 3 ingredient icons or "No maida · No refined sugar · No palm oil".
- **Product placement:** Centre, dominant.
- **CTA:** "Try a pack."
- **Text quantity:** Low.
- **Scroll-stopping because:** Premium, appetising photography. Best for retargeting and warm audiences who already know the category.

### 3. Ingredient callout
- **Headline formula:** "Everything that's inside." / "[N] ingredients. All of them you know." *(verify N)*
- **Visual hierarchy:** ① Product/pack centre → ② ingredient photos or labels radiating with thin lines → ③ headline top
- **Body copy:** Each callout is 2–4 words ("Cow butter, not palm oil").
- **Proof:** The callouts *are* the proof. They must match the back of the pack exactly.
- **Product placement:** Centre.
- **CTA:** "See the full label."
- **Text quantity:** Medium (many small labels, each short).
- **Scroll-stopping because:** Diagram-like, information-rich visuals reward a pause and feel transparent.

### 4. Meme
- **Headline formula:** A familiar meme structure adapted to the tension (use **templates we create or license**, not copyrighted film stills or real people's faces without rights).
  → *"Me: buys snacks / My dad: [squinting at label image]"*
- **Visual hierarchy:** ① Meme text/image → ② small pack in the corner or the final panel
- **Body copy:** None in the image. The caption continues the joke, then one product line.
- **Proof:** Minimal; the joke earns attention, the caption earns the click.
- **Product placement:** Small corner or final panel. The punchline can *be* the pack.
- **CTA:** Soft: "Dad-approved label. Link in bio."
- **Text quantity:** Low.
- **Scroll-stopping because:** Native to the feed. It doesn't look like an ad, and it's shareable.

### 5. Comparison
- **Headline formula:** "[Their category habit] vs [ours]." Never brand vs brand.
  → *"Your peanut butter vs peanut butter."*
- **Visual hierarchy:** ① Two columns → ② headline across the top → ③ our side visually warmer and brighter
- **Body copy:** 3–4 comparison rows, **only about our product's verified facts**. The left side should be generic ("often contains added sugar") or a blank for the viewer to fill in.
- **Proof:** Our nutrition/ingredient facts.
- **Product placement:** Right column.
- **CTA:** "Check yours. Then try ours."
- **Text quantity:** Medium.
- **Scroll-stopping because:** Comparison is instinctively engaging, because the brain wants to know "which side am I on?"
- ⚠️ No competitor logos, recognisable packaging, or unsubstantiated "their product contains X" claims. Comparative advertising can't be misleading or disparaging, so run all comparisons past review.

### 6. "Read the label"
- **Headline formula:** "Flip it." / "Our best-looking side." / "Don't read our front. Read our back."
- **Visual hierarchy:** ① The back of the pack, big and sharp → ② enlarged ingredient text beside it → ③ small headline
- **Body copy:** The ingredient list, retyped large (verbatim from the pack).
- **Proof:** The label itself.
- **Product placement:** The back of the pack IS the product placement. The front appears small in a corner.
- **CTA:** "Flip before you buy."
- **Text quantity:** Medium (the ingredient list).
- **Scroll-stopping because:** No other brand advertises its back label. It's ownable and counter-intuitive.

### 7. Product + lifestyle
- **Headline formula:** "[Occasion]. Sorted." / "[Time of day]. [Product]."
  → *"Tiffin. Sorted."*
- **Visual hierarchy:** ① Lifestyle photo with product in use (tiffin, toast, desk) → ② short headline → ③ pack small
- **Body copy:** One line of swap.
- **Proof:** The pack visible in the scene.
- **Product placement:** Integrated into the scene (in hand, in the tiffin, on the table).
- **CTA:** "Pack one tomorrow."
- **Text quantity:** Low.
- **Scroll-stopping because:** Aspirational but believable, and it helps people imagine using it.

### 8. UGC screenshot-style
- **Headline formula:** Looks like a native post or story: a creator photo with casual story-text overlay.
  → *"ok these millet cookies are actually good??"*
- **Visual hierarchy:** ① Casual phone photo (imperfect) → ② story-style text (Instagram font) → ③ pack in hand
- **Body copy:** Casual, lowercase, emoji-light.
- **Proof:** Authenticity of the image.
- **Product placement:** In hand, casual.
- **CTA:** Caption-based.
- **Text quantity:** Low.
- **Scroll-stopping because:** Blends into Stories, so the viewer's "ad radar" doesn't trigger.
- ⚠️ **Must be a real creator's real content**, used with permission and disclosed per ASCI influencer guidelines. Never fake a screenshot of a real person.

### 9. Quote / testimonial
- **Headline formula:** "[Real customer quote]" + "— [First name], [city]"
- **Visual hierarchy:** ① Large quote marks + quote → ② stars/source (if real) → ③ product
- **Body copy:** The quote only. Choose quotes about **taste, ease, ingredients, kids enjoying it**, never health outcomes.
- **Proof:** Name, city, source platform, rating *(all real; verify)*.
- **Product placement:** Bottom, or beside the quote.
- **CTA:** "Taste what they're talking about."
- **Text quantity:** Medium.
- **Scroll-stopping because:** Human words feel trustworthy. Specific quotes ("my son asked for a second") beat generic ones ("great product").

### 10. Split-screen
- **Headline formula:** "[Situation A] / [Situation B]" or "Expectation / Reality".
  → *"What I expected: 'health cookie' / What I got: [macro of cookie break]"*
- **Visual hierarchy:** ① Two halves, strong contrast (grey vs warm) → ② short labels
- **Body copy:** None beyond the labels.
- **Proof:** Visual contrast.
- **Product placement:** The "reality" / right half.
- **CTA:** "See for yourself."
- **Text quantity:** Very low.
- **Scroll-stopping because:** Instant visual contrast, readable in half a second.

### 11. Before/after belief shift (no health claims)
- **Headline formula:** "I used to think [old belief]. / Now I think [new belief]." This is about **beliefs**, never bodies or health.
  → *"I used to think: better ingredients = worse taste. / Now: [cookie photo]."*
- **Visual hierarchy:** ① Top half, "used to think", muted → ② bottom half, "now", warm with the product
- **Body copy:** None.
- **Proof:** The product image resolving the belief.
- **Product placement:** Bottom half.
- **CTA:** "Change your mind."
- **Text quantity:** Low.
- **Scroll-stopping because:** Belief shifts are satisfying mini-stories.
- ❌ **Never** body, weight, skin or health before/afters.

### 12. Cultural Indian household insight
- **Headline formula:** A universal Indian household truth, stated warmly.
  → *"Nobody in an Indian house is giving up chai-biscuit."*
- **Visual hierarchy:** ① Culturally specific photo (steel glasses, kulhad, tiffin, verandah) → ② headline → ③ pack
- **Body copy:** "So we changed the biscuit."
- **Proof:** Swaps in the caption.
- **Product placement:** In the scene.
- **CTA:** "Keep the chai. Change the biscuit."
- **Text quantity:** Low.
- **Scroll-stopping because:** Cultural recognition creates warmth, identity and shares.

### 13. Office humour
- **Headline formula:** Office-life truth + twist.
  → *"The 4 PM meeting that could've been an email. At least I have snacks."*
- **Visual hierarchy:** ① Mock (generic) calendar/Slack/email UI or a desk photo → ② the punchline → ③ pack in a drawer
- **Body copy:** None or one line.
- **Proof:** Minimal.
- **Product placement:** Desk drawer, laptop side.
- **CTA:** "Survive the 4 PM."
- **Text quantity:** Low-medium.
- **Scroll-stopping because:** Relatable workplace humour gets shared in office groups.
- ⚠️ Use generic UI, not real Slack/Teams/Google branding.

### 14. Tiffin
- **Headline formula:** "[Tiffin moment]" + outcome.
  → *"Came back empty. ✅"*
- **Visual hierarchy:** ① Top-down steel tiffin (packed or empty) → ② short headline → ③ pack beside it
- **Body copy:** "Millets, oats, jaggery. Read the back, then pack it."
- **Proof:** The label strip.
- **Product placement:** Next to or inside the tiffin.
- **CTA:** "Pack one tomorrow."
- **Text quantity:** Low.
- **Scroll-stopping because:** The steel tiffin is an instant cultural and parental trigger.

### 15. Gym
- **Headline formula:** A number or a gym truth.
  → *"25g protein / 100g. 0 added sugar."*
- **Visual hierarchy:** ① Big number typography → ② jar → ③ gym context (bag, shaker, towel) subtle
- **Body copy:** "Just peanuts doing peanut things."
- **Proof:** The nutrition panel.
- **Product placement:** Right side, large.
- **CTA:** "Fix the jar."
- **Text quantity:** Low.
- **Scroll-stopping because:** Numbers stop scrollers who track numbers.

### 16. Recipe / use case
- **Headline formula:** "[N] ways to eat [product]" / "[Product] + [familiar food] = [result]"
  → *"3 breakfasts. 1 jar."*
- **Visual hierarchy:** ① Grid (3–4 panels) of uses → ② headline → ③ jar centre
- **Body copy:** One word per panel ("Toast", "Roti roll", "Apple dip", "Smoothie").
- **Proof:** Real food photography.
- **Product placement:** Centre or in every panel.
- **CTA:** "Pick your favourite."
- **Text quantity:** Low-medium.
- **Scroll-stopping because:** Useful, save-worthy, and it expands occasions (and repeat purchases). Also works well as a **carousel**.

### 17. Close-up product appetite
- **Headline formula:** 2–4 words or none. → *"Feels like a treat."*
- **Visual hierarchy:** ① Macro food shot filling 80%+ of the frame (a cookie breaking, a spread ribbon, a PB fold) → ② tiny headline → ③ small pack
- **Body copy:** None.
- **Proof:** The texture.
- **Product placement:** Small, bottom corner.
- **CTA:** Caption only.
- **Text quantity:** Minimal.
- **Scroll-stopping because:** Appetite appeal is primal. The brain reacts to food texture before reading anything.

## Carousel system

Carousels suit education, use cases and label transparency. Default 5-card structure:

| Card | Job | Example (Ragi & Cocoa Spread) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hook (a static in its own right) | "Guess the ingredient in this chocolate spread 👀" |
| 2 | Tension / curiosity | "Hint: your grandmother cooked with it." |
| 3 | Reveal / mechanism | "Ragi. Plus peanuts, cocoa, jaggery." |
| 4 | Proof | The back of the pack, enlarged |
| 5 | Life-fit + CTA | "Toast · Roti · Apples · Spoon. Try the jar." |

Rules: card 1 must work alone. Each card has one idea. Keep visual continuity (the same background or a panorama). The final card always has the pack plus the CTA.
