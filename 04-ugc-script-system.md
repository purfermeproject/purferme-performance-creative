# Part 5 — UGC Script System

This covers real-creator UGC, AI UGC, and founder or team talking-head videos. The same structure applies to all three; only the production changes.

---

## The universal script template

Every UGC script is built from these nine blocks, in this order. Shorter durations drop or merge blocks; they never reorder them without a reason.

| Block | Framework step(s) | Job | Template line |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Hook** | 1–2 | Stop the scroll, place them in a moment | "[Specific moment/confession/claim]" |
| **2. Tension** | 3 | Name the friction | "The thing is, [honest friction in the customer's words]." |
| **3. Old solution** | 4–5 | What they do now, and the let-down | "So I used to [current workaround], but [quiet let-down]." |
| **4. Belief shift** | 6 | Dissolve the trade-off | "Then I realised [it doesn't have to be X or Y]." |
| **5. Mechanism** | 7 | The ingredient swap | "[Ingredient] instead of [usual]. [Ingredient] instead of [usual]." |
| **6. Product reveal** | 8 | Name + pack | "This is [product] from Puŕ Ferme Project." |
| **7. Proof** | 9, 12, 13 | Back-of-pack, objection answer, or review | "Here's the back. [Read list]. And before you ask, [objection answer]." |
| **8. Reaction / taste** | 10, 11, 14 | Sensory proof + life-fit + feeling | "[Bite/crunch/swirl]. [Honest reaction]. [Where it fits my day]." |
| **9. CTA** | 15 | Low-risk next step | "[Action verb] + [product] + [reason/benefit]." |

## Duration templates

Timings are approximate. Edit to the footage and let the hook breathe, but keep the **proportions**.

### 10–15 seconds — "One idea, one proof"
**Use for:** Reels and Stories prospecting, hook tests, retargeting, craving-led ads.
**Structure:** Hook → Mechanism/Reveal → Taste → CTA. Skip tension, old solution and belief shift, or imply them through the hook.

| Time | Block | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 0–2s | **Hook** | Appetite shot or pack-flip with the on-screen hook text. The pack is visible. |
| 2–6s | **Mechanism + reveal** | 2–3 swaps as quick text cards over product B-roll |
| 6–11s | **Proof + taste** | The back-of-pack flash (1.5s) plus bite/crunch/swirl with sound |
| 11–15s | **CTA** | Pack + product, the CTA line |

**Example (Breakfast Cookies):**
> **0–2s:** *[Chai dunk, slow-mo]* OST: "Keep the chai."
> **2–6s:** OST: "Change the biscuit." → "Millets + oats · Jaggery · Cow butter"
> **6–11s:** *[Pack flipped, finger on list → bite, crunch]*
> **11–15s:** OST: "Millet & Oats Breakfast Cookies. Try a pack."

---

### 20–30 seconds — "Problem, swap, proof"
**Use for:** The core prospecting workhorse for most products.
**Structure:** All nine blocks, with tension and old solution compressed.

| Time | Block | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3s | **Hook** | A creator plus a strong opening line, the pack in frame (not yet discussed) |
| 3–6s | **Tension** | One line of honest friction |
| 6–9s | **Old solution** | Generic, unbranded alternative, one line |
| 9–11s | **Belief shift** | One line ("It doesn't have to be one or the other.") |
| 11–15s | **Mechanism** | 2–3 swaps with ingredient B-roll |
| 15–17s | **Product reveal** | The pack held still, name said aloud |
| 17–21s | **Proof** | Back-of-pack close-up |
| 21–26s | **Reaction / taste** | Bite, sound, genuine reaction |
| 26–30s | **CTA** | Pack + CTA line |

---

### 30–45 seconds — "The full story"
**Use for:** Label-led and comparison angles, sceptical audiences, products needing education (Unsweetened PB, Ragi & Cocoa Spread, Sunrise Bowl).
**Structure:** All nine blocks, plus life-fit and one objection.

| Time | Block | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3s | **Hook** | As above |
| 3–8s | **Tension** | Relatable, a moment of personality |
| 8–13s | **Old solution** | The workaround and the quiet let-down, with visual evidence |
| 13–16s | **Belief shift** | The reframe |
| 16–22s | **Mechanism** | The ingredient swap sequence |
| 22–25s | **Product reveal** | The pack, the name |
| 25–30s | **Proof** | Back-of-pack plus one objection handled ("Yes, jaggery is still sugar; here's why we chose it") |
| 30–38s | **Reaction / taste + life-fit** | Taste plus 2–3 occasion cuts (tiffin, desk, chai) |
| 38–45s | **CTA** | Emotional payoff line + CTA |

---

### 45–60 seconds — "Story-led / founder / demo"
**Use for:** Founder stories, blind taste tests, family skits, recipe demos, retargeting explainers. Only use this length when the **content itself** holds attention (a story, a reveal, humour, a demo). Never pad a 30s script to reach 60s.

| Time | Block | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3s | **Hook** | Story opener ("I told my office these were regular chocolate cookies.") |
| 3–12s | **Tension** | The story set-up, characters, stakes |
| 12–20s | **Old solution** | Context, with humour or a personal detail |
| 20–25s | **Belief shift** | The turn in the story |
| 25–33s | **Mechanism** | Ingredients explained, perhaps by the founder or a sceptic reading |
| 33–37s | **Product reveal** | Pack moment |
| 37–45s | **Proof** | Back-of-pack plus a review, trust signal or traceability *(verify)* |
| 45–54s | **Reaction / taste** | Multiple reactions, life-fit, emotional payoff |
| 54–60s | **CTA** | Warm close |

## Product visibility rules

When should the pack appear, and when should the brand be named?

### Our recommendation: **show early, name late**

| Question | Answer | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Should the pack be visible in the first 2 seconds, even if not verbally introduced?** | **Yes, in most ads.** Softly placed in frame (on the counter, in hand, in the background), not held up to the camera. | Meta's creative best-practice guidance recommends featuring the product or brand early, and most viewers never reach the reveal. A pack in frame from second one means **even a 3-second view builds some brand memory**. Not announcing it keeps the ad from feeling like an ad. |
| **Should the brand be hidden until the mechanism is established?** | **The name, yes; the pack, no.** Say the name after the swap (block 6). | Naming the brand too early turns the viewer's "this is interesting" into "this is an ad" before they care. Showing the pack passively has no such cost. |
| **Exception: hide the pack entirely until the reveal** | Use only for **blind taste tests** and **"guess the ingredient"** formats. | The concealment *is* the hook. Revealing the pack early breaks the story. |
| **Exception: pack as the hook** | **Pack-flip hooks** (A-05 Flip It), **product-first statics**, and **retargeting**. | For these, the pack or label is the pattern interrupt, or the viewer already knows us. |

### Pack-on-screen standards
- **Minimum reveal hold:** 1.5 seconds, still, with the logo readable on a phone.
- **Back-of-pack hold:** 2–3 seconds, with enlarged ingredient text overlaid (real label text is too small on mobile).
- **End card:** pack + product + CTA, at most 1.5–2 seconds (viewers drop off at obvious end cards).
- **Total pack time:** aim for the product or pack to be on screen for **≥40% of the runtime**, in varied forms (in hand, on the counter, in a tiffin, on toast).
- **No glare**, no crushed or damaged packs, and always the **current** pack design.

### By route
| Route | Pack first visible | Brand first spoken |
|---|---|---|
| A. Craving-led | 0–2s (background, soft focus) | After the taste shot (≈ 30% into the ad) |
| B. Label-led | 0–2s (in hand, possibly the back label itself) | After the mechanism (≈ 50%) |
| C. Routine-led | 0–3s (in the scene: counter, drawer, tiffin) | At the life-fit moment (≈ 40%) |
| D. Humour-led | Varies; often the punchline | At the punchline or end card |

## Creator direction notes

### Casting
- Real Indian households, including joint families, dads, grandparents, working women, hostel students and gym regulars. Don't cast only young mothers.
- Match the creator to the tension: a gym regular for PB, a parent for tiffin, an office worker for 5 PM.
- For kids: parental consent, a comfortable setting, **never scripted lines forced on a child**, and follow Meta's policies on minors in ads.

### Performance
- **Talk, don't present.** It should look like a message to a friend, not a pitch.
- **Genuine reactions only.** If the creator doesn't like the product, don't use them. Don't coach them into fake delight.
- **One take of the hook in 5+ variations.** Always shoot extra hook takes (see [Part 8](07-creative-multiplication.md)).
- Hindi, English and Hinglish versions. Regional-language versions (Tamil, Kannada, Telugu, Marathi, Bengali) for regional tests. Ragi resonates especially in Karnataka and Tamil Nadu.

### Shot list every UGC shoot must capture (regardless of script)
1. 5+ hook variations to camera
2. Pack in hand, turned to camera, held still (3s)
3. Back-of-pack slow push-in (5s)
4. Product out of the pack (cookie break, jar open, porridge pour)
5. Taste: bite with audible sound, and a genuine reaction
6. 3+ life-fit occasions (tiffin, desk, chai, gym bag, breakfast table)
7. Raw ingredients B-roll, if available
8. Neutral "talking" B-roll for cutaways
9. A CTA line in 3 variations

### AI UGC-specific rules
- AI avatars **may not** claim personal experiences that didn't happen ("my son loves these") unless the ad clearly isn't presenting a real person's testimonial. Prefer **informational** AI scripts ("Here's what's in this cookie") over **testimonial** AI scripts.
- Label AI-generated content where Meta or local law requires it.
- AI visuals of the product must match the real pack and real product exactly. No AI-generated "ideal" cookies that look better than the real thing.
- Never generate AI children for testimonial-style ads.
- Use AI UGC mainly for **hook and angle testing at low cost**, then reshoot winners with real creators.
