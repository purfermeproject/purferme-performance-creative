# Part 7 — The Hook Engine

A hook has one job: **earn the next 3 seconds from the right person.** It doesn't sell the product. It sells the next line.

## How a Puŕ Ferme hook is built

Every hook combines **three layers**, and all three should be tested separately:

| Layer | What it is | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Visual hook** (first frame) | What they *see* in 0–1s | Appetite macro, pack flip, a face mid-reaction, a steel tiffin, text on a plain background |
| **Text hook** (on-screen) | What they *read* in 0–2s | "My dad read the whole label." |
| **Verbal hook** (spoken) | What they *hear* in 0–3s | "Nobody in my house is giving up chai-biscuit." |

The strongest hooks make all three say **the same thing in different ways**. Weak hooks have three unrelated layers.

## The hook formula

> **[Specific person or moment] + [tension or surprise] + [implied promise the ad will keep]**

Example: *"My kid has a sixth sense for 'healthy' food."* (person) *"This one got past it."* (surprise and implied promise: you'll see a kid enjoying it and learn what's inside.)

## The fulfilment test (anti-clickbait rule)

Before any hook is approved, answer:
1. **Does the body of the ad deliver exactly what the hook implied?** If the hook says "my dad read the whole label", we must see Dad reading it.
2. **Is it literally true?** "My son asked for a second one" must have actually happened.
3. **Would the viewer feel tricked at second 15?** If yes, kill it.
4. **Does it pass the [guardrails](12-claims-guardrails.md)?** A hook is copy, and claims rules apply.

## Hook-writing rules for our brand
- Specific beats general: "6:45 AM" beats "morning". "Steel tiffin" beats "lunchbox".
- The customer's voice beats the brand's voice: "I", "my kid", "my dad", not "we at Puŕ Ferme".
- Curiosity must pay off within 10 seconds.
- No fear verbs: *poison, toxic, killing, dangerous, harmful, chemicals*.
- No health promises in hooks, ever.
- Hinglish is allowed and encouraged where natural ("Theek hai", "Beta, what's in this?").

## Generating new hooks: the 5×5 method

Take one **tension** (T-##) and run it through five lenses, then five voices:

| Lens | Question |
|---|---|
| Moment | *When* exactly does this happen? (time, place, object) |
| Person | *Who* says it? (kid, dad, nani, colleague, gym bro) |
| Twist | What's the unexpected fact? |
| Number | Is there a verified number? (25g, 4 "no"s) |
| Emotion | What's the feeling? (relief, pride, surprise, amusement) |

| Voice | Form |
|---|---|
| Statement | "My kid has a sixth sense for 'healthy' food." |
| Question | "Does your kid have a sixth sense for 'healthy' food?" |
| POV | "POV: your kid's 'healthy food' radar goes off." |
| Confession | "I tried to sneak 'healthy' snacks past my kid. It never works." |
| Contrarian | "Don't try to sneak healthy food past your kids." |

One tension × 5 lenses × 5 voices = **25 candidate hooks**. Shortlist the 3–5 that pass the fulfilment test and fit the footage.
