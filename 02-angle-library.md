# Part 3 — Creative Angle Library

An **angle** is a specific argument for *why this product, for this person, now*. One tension can produce many angles, and one angle can produce many hooks.

**Tension (T-##) → Angle (A-##) → Hooks (H-##) → Executions (ads)**

Every angle below includes: insight, target audience, best product, hook, visual setup, body argument, proof, CTA, static version and UGC version. Tensions referenced are from [Part 2](01-consumer-tensions.md).

> ⚠️ Any line marked *(verify)* depends on a fact listed in the [verification register](13-verification-register.md). Don't run it until that fact is confirmed.

## Problem aware

### A-01 · The 5 PM Collapse
- **Insight:** Intentions don't fail at breakfast; they fail late afternoon, when you're tired and the nearest thing wins. (T-27)
- **Target audience:** Working adults 25–40, office and WFH.
- **Best product:** Millet & Oats Breakfast Cookies.
- **Hook:** "I was doing so well today. Then 5 PM happened."
- **Visual setup:** A laptop, a clock showing 4:58, a creator slumping, a hand reaching toward an empty desk area. Then the hand finds the Puŕ Ferme pack in the drawer.
- **Body argument:** "The fix isn't willpower. It's having something you actually like already in the drawer. Millets, oats, jaggery, cow butter. No maida, no palm oil."
- **Proof:** A back-of-pack flip and the crunch sound.
- **CTA:** "Stock the drawer before 5 PM does."
- **Static version:** A clock icon at 5:00 PM. Headline: "5 PM happens. Be ready." The pack in an open desk drawer.
- **UGC version:** A creator filming a "day in my life", jump-cutting to 5 PM and a dramatic sigh, then opening the drawer to show the pack, a bite, a chai sip and a nod.

### A-02 · Breakfast While Finding Keys
- **Insight:** Weekday breakfast is often something eaten while doing three other things. (T-24)
- **Target audience:** Young professionals, parents doing the school run.
- **Best product:** Breakfast Cookies (and PB toast as a secondary).
- **Hook:** "My breakfast is whatever I can eat while looking for my keys."
- **Visual setup:** A chaotic morning montage: a shoe in hand, keys missing, a phone ringing. The creator grabs two cookies from the pack while leaving.
- **Body argument:** "If breakfast is going to be hand-held anyway, I'd rather know what's in it." Then the swap list.
- **Proof:** The ingredient list on screen. Eating it in the auto or car.
- **CTA:** "For the mornings that don't go to plan."
- **Static version:** A flat-lay of keys, phone, wallet and two cookies. Headline: "Keys. Phone. Wallet. Breakfast."
- **UGC version:** POV from the door, then a mirror-selfie video running out the door, and a talk-to-camera in the car.
- ⚠️ Don't say "complete breakfast" or "balanced breakfast". It's a breakfast *cookie*, positioned for on-the-go mornings.

## Comparison

### A-03 · Same Jar, Different Inside
- **Insight:** The chocolate-spread jar is the most-opened, least-read thing in the house. (T-30)
- **Target audience:** Parents 28–42, urban households that already buy chocolate spread.
- **Best product:** Ragi & Cocoa Spread with Jaggery.
- **Hook:** "Two jars. Both chocolatey. Read the back."
- **Visual setup:** Two jars side by side: one **generic and unbranded** (label covered with plain white tape), one ours. Hands turn both round.
- **Body argument:** "I'm not here to tell you what to buy. Just read what's in yours. Ours is peanuts, ragi, cocoa, jaggery. *(Complete list: verify.)*"
- **Proof:** A close-up of our back label with the ingredients enlarged, then a spread swirl on toast.
- **CTA:** "Read yours. Then try ours."
- **Static version:** A split screen with a generic jar and a blank "Your jar's ingredients: ______" versus our jar with the full verified list.
- **UGC version:** A creator at the kitchen counter reading both labels aloud, with a genuine reaction at the length difference *(only if true; verify before filming)*.
- ⚠️ Never show or name a competitor brand. Never claim "X% less sugar than [brand]" without a lab-verified, legally reviewed comparison.

### A-04 · Two Peanut Butters
- **Insight:** Many people switched to peanut butter to eat better without checking whether theirs had added sugar. (T-32)
- **Target audience:** Gym-goers and breakfast PB users, 22–38.
- **Best product:** Unsweetened Peanut Butter.
- **Hook:** "I've been eating peanut butter for 3 years. I only just checked for added sugar."
- **Visual setup:** Two spoons of PB. The generic sweetened jar's label is blurred. Ours is shown clearly.
- **Body argument:** "Peanut butter doesn't *need* sugar. Ours has no added sugar and 25g protein per 100g."
- **Proof:** The nutrition panel close-up showing the protein line and the ingredient list.
- **CTA:** "Switch the jar, keep the habit."
- **Static version:** "Your peanut butter vs. peanut butter." The left jar is blurred, the right is ours with "No added sugar · 25g protein/100g".
- **UGC version:** A gym-bag creator, post-workout, making toast and saying "the one swap I didn't know I needed to make".

## Label-reading

### A-05 · Flip It *(brand signature angle)*
- **Insight:** Front-of-pack is marketing. Back-of-pack is the truth. Nobody has made "flip the pack" a brand behaviour. (T-10, T-11)
- **Target audience:** Ingredient-conscious buyers, broad.
- **Best product:** All. Start with Breakfast Cookies.
- **Hook:** "Don't read the front of any pack. Including ours. Flip it."
- **Visual setup:** A hand picks up our pack and immediately flips it. There's no front-of-pack shot at the start.
- **Body argument:** "Front of the pack is where brands talk. The back is where they have to be honest. Here's our back."
- **Proof:** A slow push-in on the full ingredient list.
- **CTA:** "Flip before you buy. Anything."
- **Static version:** Our back-of-pack photographed large, with the headline "Our best-looking side."
- **UGC version:** A creator in a supermarket aisle flipping random (unbranded, blurred) packs, then ours, and nodding.
- 💡 This can become a recurring series: "#FlipIt" with a different product each week.

### A-06 · The 10-Second Label
- **Insight:** A label you can read in 10 seconds is itself a benefit. (T-12)
- **Target audience:** Busy ingredient-conscious buyers.
- **Best product:** Unsweetened PB *(verify ingredient count)*, then Breakfast Cookies.
- **Hook:** "Can you read your peanut butter's ingredients in 10 seconds? Ours, you can."
- **Visual setup:** A 10-second countdown timer on screen. The creator reads our label aloud and finishes early.
- **Body argument:** "No Googling required."
- **Proof:** The real label, on screen, in full.
- **CTA:** "Short label. Long list of uses."
- **Static version:** A stopwatch graphic plus the full ingredient list in large type. "Reading time: 3 seconds."
- **UGC version:** A challenge format: "Read your jar's label out loud. I'll wait."

## Ingredient transparency

### A-07 · Meet the Ingredients
- **Insight:** People trust what they can recognise as food. Seeing the raw ingredients makes the product tangible. (T-12, T-38)
- **Target audience:** Broad. Strong for Meta prospecting.
- **Best product:** Breakfast Cookies, Ragi & Cocoa Spread.
- **Hook:** "Every single thing that goes into our cookie. On one plate."
- **Visual setup:** An overhead shot of a steel thali. Each raw ingredient is placed in its own katori: millets, oats, nuts, jaggery, cow butter. *(Use only verified ingredients, in their real form.)*
- **Body argument:** "That's it. Things you already have in your kitchen, baked into a cookie."
- **Proof:** Match the plate to the back-of-pack list on screen.
- **CTA:** "Same ingredients. Zero baking."
- **Static version:** The thali flat-lay with ingredient labels and the pack in the centre.
- **UGC version:** Stop-motion with the ingredients "jumping" into the pack.
- ⚠️ "Things you already have in your kitchen" is only true if every ingredient is a common kitchen ingredient. Check the verified list first.

### A-08 · What We Left Out
- **Insight:** Sometimes what isn't there speaks louder. The four "no"s on Breakfast Cookies are concrete and easy to understand.
- **Target audience:** Label-aware parents, adults.
- **Best product:** Millet & Oats Breakfast Cookies.
- **Hook:** "The most important ingredients in this cookie are the four we didn't use."
- **Visual setup:** Four empty bowls, labelled "maida", "refined sugar", "palm oil", "preservatives". A hand removes each.
- **Body argument:** "What we used instead: millets and oats, jaggery, cow butter."
- **Proof:** Back-of-pack.
- **CTA:** "Taste what's left."
- **Static version:** "No maida. No refined sugar. No palm oil. No preservatives." in large type, with a cookie.
- **UGC version:** A creator counting the "no"s on their fingers with dramatic pauses.
- ⚠️ "No preservatives" is **yellow**. See [guardrails](12-claims-guardrails.md#no-preservatives).

## Taste surprise

### A-09 · The Blind Taste Test
- **Insight:** "Better ingredients" makes people expect worse taste. A blind test removes the bias. (T-29, T-33)
- **Target audience:** Sceptics. Works as prospecting.
- **Best product:** Chocolate Cookies, Ragi & Cocoa Spread.
- **Hook:** "I told my office these were regular chocolate cookies."
- **Visual setup:** Office or home. Colleagues or family taste, react, then learn what's inside.
- **Body argument:** Their unscripted reactions carry it. The reveal shows the pack and the back label.
- **Proof:** Real reactions, **unscripted, uncut in meaning**, with consent.
- **CTA:** "Try it on your own sceptics."
- **Static version:** A reaction-face photo plus a quote: "Wait, this is millets?" (a real quote only).
- **UGC version:** A hidden-camera-lite set-up with a friend group or family.
- ⚠️ Don't claim "tastes exactly like [brand]" or "indistinguishable". Let reactions speak. If reactions are mixed, show the good ones honestly, but never stage them.

### A-10 · The Chai Dunk Test
- **Insight:** In India, a biscuit's true test is the chai dunk. It's a culturally loaded, visual proof of texture. (T-22)
- **Target audience:** Broad Indian households, 25–55.
- **Best product:** Breakfast Cookies.
- **Hook:** "Every cookie has to pass one test in this house."
- **Visual setup:** A slow-motion dunk in a steel glass or kulhad of chai, the pull out, the bite. Sound up.
- **Body argument:** "Millets, oats, jaggery, cow butter, and it survives the dunk." *(Verify dunk performance on camera before claiming.)*
- **Proof:** The dunk itself, on camera.
- **CTA:** "Chai's ready. Is your cookie?"
- **Static version:** A macro shot mid-dunk with chai dripping. Headline: "Passed the only test that matters."
- **UGC version:** A grandparent as the "judge" giving a verdict.

## Parenting

### A-11 · The Yes-Parent
- **Insight:** Parents are tired of saying no. The payoff is saying yes without the internal debate. (T-02)
- **Target audience:** Parents of kids 4–12.
- **Best product:** Chocolate Cookies.
- **Hook:** "My kid asked for chocolate cookies and I said yes without reading anything first. Because I already had."
- **Visual setup:** A kid asking at the kitchen counter. The parent says "yes" instantly, and the kid looks shocked.
- **Body argument:** "I read the label once. That was enough." Then the ingredients.
- **Proof:** Back-of-pack.
- **CTA:** "Be the yes-parent for a day."
- **Static version:** A kid's shocked face. Text: "Wait… you said YES?"
- **UGC version:** Parent-creator comedic skit.
- ⚠️ Ingredient list and sweetener for Chocolate Cookies **(verify)** before production.

### A-12 · Nani vs Mummy, Settled
- **Insight:** Grandparents give treats, parents worry, kids win. A product both approve of ends the tug-of-war. (T-03)
- **Target audience:** Parents in joint or visiting-grandparent families.
- **Best product:** Chocolate Cookies, Breakfast Cookies.
- **Hook:** "Nani brings sweets. Mummy checks labels. This is the one thing they both agreed on."
- **Visual setup:** A light-hearted two-generation scene. Both grandparent and parent inspect the pack, then nod.
- **Body argument:** Nani approves of the ingredients (millets, jaggery, butter: "like the old days"), mummy approves of the label.
- **Proof:** The ingredient list plus both "judges" eating.
- **CTA:** "Family-approved. Both generations."
- **Static version:** Two tick-boxes: "Nani ✅ Mummy ✅".
- **UGC version:** A real multi-generational family.

## Ingredients in plain sight

### A-13 · Wait, There's Ragi in This?
- **Insight:** Ragi is familiar and culturally trusted but rarely associated with chocolate. The surprise is the hook. (T-05, T-29)
- **Target audience:** Parents, millennials familiar with ragi from childhood.
- **Best product:** Ragi & Cocoa Spread.
- **Hook:** "My mother fed me ragi every morning. I did not expect to find it in a chocolate spread."
- **Visual setup:** A nostalgic ragi-malt glass, cut to a chocolatey spread swirl.
- **Body argument:** "Peanuts, ragi, cocoa, jaggery. Chocolatey, and made differently."
- **Proof:** Back-of-pack plus texture shot.
- **CTA:** "Ragi, the way you didn't expect it."
- **Static version:** "Ragi 🤝 Chocolate" plus the jar.
- **UGC version:** A creator doing a "you won't guess the ingredient" game with a family member.
- ⚠️ No claims about ragi's calcium or other nutrients unless the product's *own* verified nutrition panel supports them and the claim meets FSSAI thresholds.

### A-14 · The Cookie That Doesn't Look Like a Lesson
- **Insight:** Kids reject food that looks "healthy". The winning snack looks like a treat. (T-04, T-07)
- **Target audience:** Parents of 4–12 year olds.
- **Best product:** Chocolate Cookies.
- **Hook:** "My kid has a sixth sense for 'healthy' food. This one got past it."
- **Visual setup:** A kid scanning a cookie suspiciously like a detective, then eating it.
- **Body argument:** "Millets and oats inside. *(Verify.)* It just looks like a chocolate cookie."
- **Proof:** The kid's real reaction and the back-of-pack.
- **CTA:** "Pass the kid test."
- **Static version:** A kid with a magnifying glass over a cookie. "Suspicious. Then gone."
- **UGC version:** A parent narrates while the kid "investigates".
- ⚠️ Don't frame it as "tricking" kids into eating healthy food. Keep it on "they enjoy it; you've read the label".

## Contrarian

### A-27 · Don't Eat This for Your Health
- **Insight:** Audiences are exhausted by health promises. Refusing to make one is disarming and credible. (T-10, T-37)
- **Target audience:** Sceptical, label-literate buyers.
- **Best product:** Breakfast Cookies, Chocolate Cookies.
- **Hook:** "Please don't buy our cookies for your health."
- **Visual setup:** A creator deadpan to camera, holding the pack.
- **Body argument:** "It's a cookie. Buy it because it tastes good. We just made it with millets, oats and jaggery, and left out maida and palm oil. That's the whole pitch."
- **Proof:** Back-of-pack.
- **CTA:** "Buy it for the taste. Read the back for peace of mind."
- **Static version:** "It's a cookie. Not a cure." plus a product photo.
- **UGC version:** The founder or a creator doing an honesty rant.
- 💡 Strongest brand-differentiating angle. Protect its credibility by never making health claims elsewhere.

### A-28 · Yes, Jaggery Is Still Sugar
- **Insight:** Savvy buyers know jaggery is still sugar. Admitting it first builds more trust than dodging it.
- **Target audience:** Label-literate adults, fitness-aware.
- **Best product:** Breakfast Cookies, Ragi & Cocoa Spread.
- **Hook:** "Someone commented 'jaggery is still sugar'. You're right. Here's why we use it anyway."
- **Visual setup:** A reply-to-comment format (use a real comment only, or a generic paraphrase shown as generic).
- **Body argument:** "We're not claiming sugar-free. We chose jaggery over refined sugar because [real reason from founders: taste, tradition, less processing? **verify**]."
- **Proof:** The label shows jaggery clearly. No hiding.
- **CTA:** "Honest cookie. Try it."
- **Static version:** "Jaggery is still sugar. We know. We still chose it." plus the pack.
- **UGC version:** Founder reply video.
- ⚠️ **Do not** claim jaggery is "healthier", "has lower GI" or is "better for blood sugar". **RED.**

## Social proof

### A-29 · Reading Our Reviews Out Loud
- **Insight:** Real customer words are more persuasive than ours, and reading them aloud adds warmth. (T-13)
- **Target audience:** Retargeting, warm audiences.
- **Best product:** Whichever has the most real reviews *(verify)*.
- **Hook:** "We read every review. Here are the ones that made us smile."
- **Visual setup:** A team member or founder on the sofa reading reviews from a phone. Screenshots appear on screen.
