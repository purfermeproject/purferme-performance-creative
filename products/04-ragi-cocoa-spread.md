# Ragi & Cocoa Peanut/Nut Spread with Jaggery

**Campaign idea:** *Chocolatey. Built differently.*
**Role in the portfolio:** The "surprise" product. A familiar indulgence with an unexpected Indian heritage ingredient (ragi). It's the most distinctive product, and the one most likely to generate curiosity and shares.
**Default route:** A (craving-led) for kids and families, B (label-led) against the conventional chocolate-spread habit.

## Verified facts we can use
- Core ingredients: peanuts, ragi, cocoa, jaggery

## Requires verification before use
- **Exact nut base**: peanut only, or mixed nuts? (The brief says "peanut/nut". The company's traceability page name suggests "choco ragi peanut butter", so confirm.)
- Full ingredient list (oil? salt? milk solids? emulsifier?)
- Whether ragi is sprouted, roasted or flour, and in what proportion
- Nutrition panel
- No palm oil? No preservatives? (Don't assume; confirm)
- Texture (smooth? spreadable straight from the jar? needs a stir?)
- Jar sizes, price

---

### A. Primary audience
Parents (28–42) in urban households that already buy a mainstream chocolate spread for kids' breakfast and snacks.

### B. Secondary audience
- Adults who love chocolate spread and eat it by the spoon.
- Millennials nostalgic for ragi (grew up on ragi malt or ragi porridge).
- PB buyers who want a chocolatey variant.

### C. Main consumer problem
The chocolate-spread jar is loved and used constantly, but few people have ever read its label. When they do, they wish there were a version that kept the chocolatey pleasure with a formulation they understand.

### D. Main desire
*"Keep the chocolatey spread everyone loves at breakfast, with ingredients I recognise."*

### E. Current alternative
- Mainstream hazelnut/cocoa spreads
- Chocolate-flavoured peanut butters
- Jam
- No spread (plain toast or roti, which kids resist)

### F. Why that alternative may disappoint
- Parents feel mild unease each time they spread it but keep buying because kids love it.
- Healthier-positioned spreads often compromise on taste and get rejected.

### G. Product mechanism
**Chocolatey pleasure from cocoa, a base of peanuts and ragi, sweetened with jaggery.** A familiar format, rebuilt from recognisable ingredients.

### H. Strongest proof
1. **"Wait, there's ragi in this?"**: the ingredient surprise reaction.
2. **The back of the pack**: a short, recognisable list *(verify)*.
3. **Texture and swirl shots**: the knife pull, the spread on warm toast, a roti roll-up.
4. **A kid's reaction.**

### I. Emotional payoff
**Delight and cleverness.** *"I found the version that works for all of us."* A small, smart-parent pride.

### M. Best 3 static ad concepts
1. **Ingredient equation.** "Peanuts + Ragi + Cocoa + Jaggery = 🍫". Raw ingredients in a row, jar at the end.
2. **"Ragi 🤝 Chocolate."** A bold, playful, meme-style static with the jar.
3. **The knife pull.** A macro texture shot. Headline: "Chocolatey. Built differently."

### N. Best CTA styles
- Curiosity: "Taste the ragi surprise."
- Breakfast: "Upgrade tomorrow's toast."
- Comparison: "Read yours. Then try ours."
- Trial: "One jar. See who finishes it first."

### O. Claims to avoid
- ❌ "Healthy Nutella alternative" (names a brand; also "healthy")
- ❌ "Rich in calcium / iron from ragi" (unless the product's own panel supports it and meets FSSAI thresholds; **verify**)
- ❌ "Good for kids' bones / growth / brain"
- ❌ "Low sugar" / "sugar-free" (jaggery is sugar)
- ❌ "Guilt-free", "healthier than regular spreads"
- ❌ Any comparative nutrition claim vs a named or recognisable competitor
- ⚠️ Peanut (and possibly other nut) allergen consideration
