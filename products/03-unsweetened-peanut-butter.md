# Unsweetened Peanut Butter

**Campaign idea:** *Peanut butter that tastes like peanuts.*
**Role in the portfolio:** The most rational, most label-driven product. It speaks to fitness and breakfast audiences, is highly repeatable (a pantry staple), and is a strong entry into the brand for adults without kids.
**Default route:** B (label-led) for prospecting, C (routine-led) for breakfast/gym.

## Verified facts we can use
- 25g protein per 100g
- No added sugar
- Peanut-forward, simple formulation

## Requires verification before use
- **Full ingredient list.** Is it 100% peanuts, or peanuts + salt, or peanuts + oil? ("Only peanuts" / "single ingredient" claims depend on this.)
- Texture: crunchy, creamy, or both variants?
- Whether it contains a stabiliser, and whether natural oil separation occurs (needed for the "oil on top" education angle)
- Roast level (for taste descriptors like "dark roasted")
- Protein per serving and the serving size on pack
- Whether the traceability QR (the company runs a traceability page for peanut butter) is live and linked from current packs
- Jar sizes, price

---

### A. Primary audience
Gym-goers and active adults, 20–35, who already eat peanut butter and track protein.

### B. Secondary audience
- Breakfast-toast households switching from jam or sweetened PB.
- Adults cutting down on added sugar generally (not medically; **no diabetes targeting**).
- Home bakers and smoothie makers who want an unsweetened base.
- Parents who use PB for kids and want to control sweetness themselves.

### C. Main consumer problem
Many people bought peanut butter as a "better" choice and later discovered theirs has added sugar (and sometimes other additions). They feel mildly let down by a choice they thought was sensible.

### D. Main desire
*"Just peanut butter. Real peanut taste, sensible numbers, nothing I didn't sign up for."*

### E. Current alternative
- Sweetened / "classic" peanut butter
- Flavoured peanut butters (chocolate, honey)
- Protein powders and bars
- Eggs and paneer as protein sources (not competitors so much as meal context)

### F. Why that alternative may disappoint
- Sweetened PB: sugar the buyer never consciously chose.
- Flavoured PB: more of a dessert than a staple.
- Protein supplements: feel like supplements, not food.
- Past unsweetened PB they tried: dry, bland or hard to spread (the taste/texture objection we must overcome).

### G. Product mechanism
**Nothing added to make it sweet. Peanuts do the talking.** No added sugar, and a peanut-forward formulation. Protein comes from the peanuts themselves (25g/100g).

### H. Strongest proof
1. The **nutrition panel**: 25g protein/100g, and the sugar line showing no added sugar.
2. The **ingredient list** *(the shorter it is, the stronger; verify)*.
3. **Texture shots**: the spoon fold, the thick swirl, the knife drag.
4. **Traceability scan** *(verify)*.

### I. Emotional payoff
**Quiet competence.** *"One less thing in my routine I need to second-guess."*

### M. Best 3 static ad concepts
1. **The number static.** Huge "25g" typography, sub "protein per 100g · no added sugar", the jar beside it, clean off-white background.
2. **The spoon.** A macro of a thick PB fold on a spoon. Headline: "Tastes like peanuts. Because it is."
3. **Label vs label.** A blurred generic jar vs our jar. "Your peanut butter vs peanut butter."

### N. Best CTA styles
- Switch: "Switch the jar. Keep the habit."
- Rational: "Check the label. Then check out."
- Routine: "Make it your post-gym toast."
- Subscription / repeat *(if available, verify)*: "Never run out."

### O. Claims to avoid
- ❌ "Builds muscle", "boosts recovery", "improves performance", "for gains"
- ❌ "Sugar-free" (peanuts contain naturally occurring sugars; use "no added sugar")
- ❌ "Diabetic-friendly", "safe for diabetics", "good for blood sugar"
- ❌ "Helps weight loss", "fat-burning", "keeps you lean"
- ❌ "Heart-healthy", "good fats for your heart"
- ❌ "100% peanuts" / "single ingredient" (until the ingredient list is verified)
- ❌ "Highest protein peanut butter" (a superlative needing market-wide substantiation)
- ⚠️ "High protein" / "rich in protein" is **yellow**. It must meet FSSAI thresholds *(verify)*. "25g protein per 100g" stated as a fact is safer than an adjective.
- ⚠️ Always consider a peanut allergen note where relevant.
