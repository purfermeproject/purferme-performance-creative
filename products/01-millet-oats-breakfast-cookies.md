# Millet & Oats Breakfast Cookies

**Campaign idea:** *Keep the chai. Change the biscuit.*
**Role in the portfolio:** Everyday hero and acquisition workhorse. Most occasions, broadest audience, clearest swap story.
**Default route:** C (routine-led) and B (label-led). See the [master framework](../00-master-framework.md#the-four-routes-through-the-framework).

## Verified facts we can use
- Made with millets and oats
- Contains nuts
- Sweetened with jaggery
- Made with cow butter
- No maida · no refined sugar · no palm oil · no preservatives *(the last one is yellow; see guardrails)*

## Requires verification before use
- Which millets, and in what proportion ("made with millets" vs "X% millets")
- Which nuts (needed for allergen copy too)
- Full ingredient list, including leavening agents, salt and any flavours
- Nutrition panel per 100g and per cookie (fibre, protein, sugar values)
- Cookie weight, pack sizes, price per cookie
- Shelf life, and how it's achieved without preservatives
- Whether it holds up to a chai dunk (test on camera before claiming)

---

### A. Primary audience
Urban working adults and parents, 25–45, who already buy packaged biscuits for chai, tiffin or the office, and have started reading labels.

### B. Secondary audience
- Grandparents and older adults who value traditional grains (millets) and jaggery.
- Hybrid and office workers building a desk-drawer stash.
- Gifting buyers (Diwali and festive hampers), if the pack is gift-worthy.

### C. Main consumer problem
The everyday biscuit (chai, tiffin, 4 PM) is a habit no one wants to give up. But most people have never read what's in it, and the "healthy" versions they've tried were disappointing to eat.

### D. Main desire
*"Let me keep my biscuit ritual, with something I'd be happy to read the back of."*

### E. Current alternative
- Regular packaged biscuits (glucose, digestive, cream, "oats" variants)
- "Health" biscuits bought once and not repurchased
- Homemade snacks when there's time (rare on weekdays)
- Skipping the snack, then overeating later

### F. Why that alternative may disappoint
- Regular biscuits: front says "oats" or "digestive", but the back usually lists refined flour and refined sugar first (general category observation; **never name a brand**).
- "Health" biscuits: dry, bland, and the family doesn't finish the pack.
- Homemade: not realistic on weekdays.

### G. Product mechanism (the swap)
| Usual | Ours |
|---|---|
| Maida | Millets + oats |
| Refined sugar | Jaggery |
| Palm oil / vanaspati | Cow butter |
| Preservatives | None *(verify)* |
| — | Nuts |

**The story in one line:** *Four ingredients swapped, one ritual kept.*

### H. Strongest proof
1. The **back of the pack**, since four "no"s are visible and checkable.
2. The **chai dunk** on camera (sensory proof of a proper biscuit).
3. The **raw ingredient thali** (millets, oats, nuts, jaggery, butter).

### I. Emotional payoff
Calm continuity. *"Nothing about my chai time had to change except what's in the biscuit."*

### M. Best 3 static ad concepts
1. **"Keep the chai. Change the biscuit."** Two steel chai glasses, cookies on a saucer, the pack behind. Warm evening light.
2. **"4 things we left out."** Large typographic ✕ list (maida, refined sugar, palm oil, preservatives) above a macro shot of a broken cookie showing texture. The pack in the bottom right.
3. **"Our best-looking side."** A photo of the back of the pack, ingredients enlarged beside it. Minimal.

### N. Best CTA styles
- Ritual-based: "Keep the chai. Change the biscuit."
- Trial: "Try one pack this week."
- Occasion: "Stock the drawer before 5 PM does." / "Pack one tomorrow."
- Label-based: "Flip it. Then try it."

### O. Claims to avoid
- ❌ "Healthy breakfast" / "complete breakfast" / "balanced breakfast" / "breakfast replacement"
- ❌ "Diabetic-friendly", "sugar-free", "low GI", "good for blood sugar"
- ❌ "Keeps you full for hours", "sustained energy", "boosts focus"
- ❌ "High fibre", "rich in protein" (unless the nutrition panel meets FSSAI thresholds; **verify**)
- ❌ "Guilt-free"
- ❌ "Healthier than regular biscuits" (comparative, unsubstantiated)
- ⚠️ "Breakfast cookie" is the product name. Use it as a name, not as a nutritional promise.
