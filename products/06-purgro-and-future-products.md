# PurGro Bars and Future Functional Snacks

**Status:** Future product. **No product facts are verified yet.**
This file is a **pre-launch framework**: the questions to answer and the rules to follow *before* making ads, so future products fit the system rather than breaking it.

> "Functional snack" is the riskiest positioning the brand will take on, because the category norm is to make benefit claims (energy, protein, focus, recovery). The brand's credibility depends on **not** adopting those norms without substantiation.

---

## Launch readiness questions

Answer all of these in writing before the first brief:

| # | Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | What is the full, final ingredient list? | Every mechanism and proof step depends on it |
| 2 | What does the nutrition panel say per bar and per 100g? | Decides which yellow claims are even possible |
| 3 | Which FSSAI nutrient-content claims does it qualify for, if any? | "Source of protein" / "high protein" / "high fibre" have regulatory thresholds |
| 4 | What is the **swap** vs the category norm? (e.g. "dates instead of…", "no protein isolate", etc.) | Our mechanism must be a visible ingredient swap, not a biological effect |
| 5 | What is the primary **occasion**? (pre-gym? travel? 4 PM? kids' after-school?) | Occasion drives the angle, not "function" |
| 6 | How does it taste compared with the bars people already buy? | Taste is still the #1 barrier |
| 7 | What does "PurGro" mean, and does the name itself imply a claim ("growth")? | ⚠️ **"Gro" may imply growth, which is a RED claim for children's products.** Get a legal opinion on the name, especially if kids are a target audience. |
| 8 | Who is it for: adults, kids, or both? | Children's food carries stricter rules |

### C. Main consumer problem (hypothesis)
Snack and protein bars often feel like supplements disguised as food: long ingredient lists, sweeteners people can't pronounce, chalky texture.
*(Tension T-19: "Gym food is either chalky, sweetened with something I can't pronounce, or boring.")*

### D. Main desire (hypothesis)
*"A bar that tastes like food, with a label I can read, that fits into my day."*

### H. Strongest proof (likely)
Back-of-pack, a texture/break shot, and a taste reaction. Plus the traceability of a key ingredient, if available.

### I. Emotional payoff (hypothesis)
*"Snack that fits my goals without feeling like a supplement."*

### J. Hook directions (placeholders; finalise only after facts are verified)
1. "A bar you can read the back of without Google."
2. "I stopped buying bars that taste like protein powder."
3. "Real food, shaped like a bar."
4. "What's in your gym bag bar? Flip it."
5. "Snack bar ingredients shouldn't need a glossary."

### K. Best angles to adapt
A-05 Flip It · A-23 The Macro You Forgot (adapted) · A-27 Don't Eat This for Your Health · A-18 The Desk Drawer Restock · A-09 Blind Taste Test.

### L–N. UGC storyline, statics, CTAs — *build at launch using the [brief template](../10-creative-brief-template.md)*

### O. Claims to avoid (already known)
- ❌ "Energy bar" (implies an energy claim; use it only if legally cleared as a descriptor)
- ❌ "Boosts energy / stamina / focus / recovery"
- ❌ "Builds muscle", "for growth" (especially for kids)
- ❌ "Meal replacement"
- ❌ "Protein bar" unless it qualifies under FSSAI protein-claim thresholds *(verify)*
- ❌ "Guilt-free", "healthy"
- ⚠️ The **name "PurGro"** itself requires legal review for implied growth claims (see question 7).

## Rule for any future product
A new product is only added to the ad system once it has:
1. a verified ingredient list and nutrition panel in the [verification register](../13-verification-register.md)
2. a completed product file in this folder (A–O)
3. at least one tension (T-##) and three angles (A-##) mapped to it
4. a claims review against the [guardrails](../12-claims-guardrails.md)
