# Sunrise Bowl Sprouted Ragi & Sweet Potato Porridge

**Campaign idea:** *Familiar ingredients. Weekday-friendly mornings.*
**Role in the portfolio:** Parent trust. It carries the **highest claim and regulatory sensitivity** of any product.
**Default route:** C (routine-led). No fear, no guilt, no comparison, no let-down step.

> ## 🛑 Read this before producing any ad for this product
>
> **1. Infant-food regulation.** In India, advertising of foods marketed for infants (under 2 years) is restricted under the **Infant Milk Substitutes, Feeding Bottles and Infant Foods (Regulation of Production, Supply and Distribution) Act, 1992 (IMS Act)**. Infant foods also fall under the FSSAI regulations for foods for infant nutrition. **Before running any paid ads**, confirm with a legal/regulatory advisor:
> - the product's licensed category and the **age group stated on the pack**
> - whether paid promotion is permitted for that age group and category
> - any mandatory statements or restrictions
>
> **2. If the product is labelled or intended for children under 2:** do **not** run paid ads until legal clearance is obtained. Creative for that audience may be prohibited entirely.
>
> **3. If the product is labelled for older children (e.g. toddlers 2+) or families:** proceed using this framework, keeping imagery and targeting aligned with the labelled age. Never show infants.
>
> *(Age suitability: **requires verification**.)*

## Verified facts we can use
- Name: Sunrise Bowl Sprouted Ragi & Sweet Potato Porridge
- Contains sprouted ragi and sweet potato

## Requires verification before use
- **Labelled age group** (critical; see above)
- Full ingredient list (anything besides sprouted ragi and sweet potato? milk solids? sweetener? salt?)
- Whether it has added sugar or salt
- Preparation method (water/milk? cook time? just add hot water?)
- Nutrition panel
- Pack size, servings per pack, price
- Allergens
- FSSAI licence category

### A. Primary audience
Parents of young children (in the **labelled age range only**), 26–38, urban, who value traditional grains like ragi but have little weekday morning time.

### B. Secondary audience
- Grandparents who buy for grandchildren.
- Adults who want a warm, simple porridge for themselves (if the product is labelled for general consumption; **verify**).

### C. Main consumer problem
Parents want to give their children a familiar, traditional-style breakfast like the ragi porridge many of them grew up with, but weekday mornings don't allow sprouting, drying, grinding and cooking from scratch. Packaged options often have ingredient lists they don't fully understand.

### D. Main desire
*"A breakfast I understand completely, that I can make quickly, and that feels like something my mother would have made."*

### E. Current alternative
- Home-made ragi porridge / ragi malt (time-heavy)
- Packaged cereal-based kids' breakfast products
- Whatever's quickest (bread, biscuits)

### F. Why that alternative may disappoint
- Home-made: the effort is unrealistic every weekday.
- Packaged: parents are unsure what's in them.
- **Tone note:** do not criticise other baby or kid foods. Parents of young children are extremely guilt-sensitive, so any implication that they've been feeding their child "wrongly" will backfire and may be unethical.

### G. Product mechanism
**Traditional-style ingredients, done ahead of time.** Ragi is sprouted, then combined with sweet potato, so the parent only has to prepare it *(verify the prep method)*.
⚠️ Do **not** attribute nutritional or digestive benefits to sprouting. Say *what* was done (sprouted), not *what it does to the body*.

### H. Strongest proof
1. **The ingredient list**: short and recognisable *(verify)*.
2. **A real preparation demo**, timed on screen *(verify)*.
3. **Parent-to-parent testimonials** about ease and ingredients (never about child health outcomes).
4. **Sourcing / making footage**: sprouted ragi, sweet potatoes.

### I. Emotional payoff
**Calm confidence.** *"I know exactly what's in my child's bowl, and my morning just got easier."*

### M. Best 3 static ad concepts
1. **The calm bowl.** A warm, steaming bowl in soft morning light, a wooden spoon, the pack beside it. Headline: "Familiar ingredients. Weekday-friendly mornings."
2. **Ingredient honesty.** A ragi grain and sweet potato shown raw, the pack between them. "What's inside: exactly what it says." *(Verify that the list truly is just these.)*
3. **Then and now.** Left: grandmother's steel vessel. Right: our pack. "The ragi she made. The mornings we have."

### N. Best CTA styles
- Routine: "Make tomorrow morning easier."
- Trust: "Read the pack. Then decide."
- Soft trial: "Try one pack this week."
- ❌ Avoid urgency CTAs entirely for this product.

### O. Claims to avoid — the strictest list in the portfolio
- ❌ **Any growth, development, brain, bone, height, weight-gain or immunity claim**
- ❌ "Complete nutrition", "balanced meal", "everything your child needs"
- ❌ "Easy to digest", "gentle on tummy", "improves digestion" (sprouting benefits are **RED**)
- ❌ "Rich in calcium / iron" (unless the panel substantiates it, FSSAI thresholds are met and legal clears it)
- ❌ "Recommended by paediatricians / doctors" (unless verified and permitted)
- ❌ "Better than homemade" / "as good as breast milk" or any comparison to breastfeeding (**prohibited**)
- ❌ "Suitable from X months" (unless it exactly matches the label and is legally cleared)
- ❌ Any imagery of infants if the product isn't cleared for infant promotion
- ❌ Guilt-based messaging ("Are you sure what you're feeding your child is safe?")
